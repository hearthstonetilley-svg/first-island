At tick 1,800:

| 99 | 35 | 21 | 0 | 0 | 30 | 4 | 0 | 1542 |
| 2026 | 32 | 23 | 1 | 0 | 22 | 5 | 0 | 1409 |

These are overlapping failure counts, not a partition. The collapse is inadequate replacement reproduction, followed by lost mate availability as cohorts die. It is not widespread health or warmth failure: those gates barely block this first reproductive collapse, and almost all ultimate deaths are old age. Births do continue sporadically beyond the initial boom; they do not replace the dying population.

The source explains the observed pressure: rest must exceed 45 to reproduce, but resting starts below 30, leaving about 536 active ticks reproductively excluded before seeking rest. Energy likewise must exceed 70, while food seeking starts below 65. Both partners must overlap in readiness and be within 1.05 units, but ordinary socialising follows the closest body regardless of its reproductive readiness. With lives only 1.5–1.9 dial intervals long, intermittent eligible encounters cannot sustain the population. The later table shows own-ready survivors with no eligible mate as populations shrink. The next fix should align restorative decisions with these gates, allow healthy bodies to approach eligible partners, and implement the requested longer lifespan and measured biological pace. It should not waive health/needs or inject population.

Measured mean parent age is 1,263–1,721 ticks, rather than one third of 1,800. The review's inference from maturity age alone overstates the birth-pace error. Lifespan does remain below its requested 2.5–3 dial interval. Parent-age events allow the correction to be checked rather than inferred from the HUD counter.

All five seeds already achieve a real grandchild birth (ticks 906/1107/594/757/1406), but all finish at zero. The corrected viability eval therefore fails **0/5**, as required. The first grandchild can occur while the HUD clock still says Generation 1.
