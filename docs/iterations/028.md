# Iteration 028: measure play cadence and restore quality

Scope: the owner's real-hardware follow-up to [Brief 2.2](../briefs/BRIEF-2.2-device-lost.md), after device-loss recovery in [027](027.md).

## Cause and changes

The owner reports WebGPU at roughly 10 fps behind the loader, then 80–100 fps after Enter. The readiness loop waited for GPU completion after every render, serializing work that normal play submits asynchronously. Quality also sampled those loading frames and could only decrease. Those measurements were not representative of play; the software-only rationale in 026 did not hold on the owner's hardware.

- Readiness now uses the same animated render and RAF scheduling as play, with no per-frame GPU completion waits. One-time shader compilation and bounded GPU warm-up remain before readiness.
- While measuring, the canvas has opacity 1 with no opacity transition. Its overlay is a flat translucent scrim with no full-screen gradients, decorative pattern, blur, filters or glowing stage dots. The canvas participates in composition rather than being hidden or completely occluded. The progress UI remains readable; the completion fade starts only upon entry.
- Keep initial high quality throughout preparation, readiness and the completion fade. Quality windows start only after the overlay has been removed, for automatic and manual entry alike. A slow readiness window offers Enter immediately instead of waiting for destructive quality reductions.
- Re-measure continuously in play. A two-second window with median above 25 ms lowers one step; three consecutive fast two-second windows (median at most 18 ms, p95 at most 25 ms) restore one step. A gap between the thresholds and the longer restoration window avoid reacting to a brief improvement. Visibility/resize resets discard partial windows and accumulated headroom.
- Low → balanced restores AO; balanced → high restores the original pixel ratio. Capture the original AO draw closure so selecting it actually restores the graph, rather than reusing the already-selected AO-free draw function. Never raise resolution above the initial device/debug cap. Post-processing-disabled mode has one resolution step and never claims AO.
- Preserve the device-loss controller, honest stopped-renderer HUD and same-world WebGL2 recovery.
